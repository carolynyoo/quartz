"Bad good" art style from Japan, favoring naive and childlike art over polish. Encourages people to draw, feel momentum and expansion

Origins from "anti-illustration" around 1980, pioneered by Teruhiko Yumura of Flamingo Studio

Teruhiko Yumura image hierarchy:
* Heta-uma: unskilled good
* Heta-heta: unskilled bad
* Uma-uma: skilled good
* Uma-heta: skilled bad

> _Uma-Uma_, it's something like a professional-looking picture made by a professional, in short, it's boring. _Uma-Heta_ illustration is the worst. It relies only on technique and has no passions, because it was produced in conscious effort to make it as good-looking as possible. When this effort happens to result in a _Heta-Heta_ image, there's very little difference from a _Heta-Uma_ illustration. We must not underestimate _Heta-Heta_ illustrations.

Uma-uma: so much of American commercial illustration falls into this
Uma-heta: obsession with learning realism as skill and valorizing it because we can't see or honor merit of art beyond it replicating what we already know

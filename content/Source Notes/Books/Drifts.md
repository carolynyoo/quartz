Author: Kate Zambreno
Read Date: 7/8/2026
## Quick Review
Enjoyed this diaristic "novel" about a woman struggling to write her book very much. Definitely a book for writers or artists—people engaging with the creative process and therefore equally fascinated and tortured by it. Appeals to the commonplace book lovers and Rilke lovers. Kate always has a notebook with her and it shows. 

The amount of correspondence and literary friendships was gorgeous and I'm inspired to identify the Sofia to my Kate (or am I the Sofia?)

* "What prevents a book from being written becomes the book itself"—found in my notebook, citing the notebooks of Camus, who was himself quoting Marcus Aurelius
* I like the idea of yoga being about a practice, about not knowing. That's what writing is for me. A ritual. **My impulse to write was private, was the way that I was silent**, or not silent, in the face of capitalism, desire, the family. 
* Was being a writer a way of escaping from having a job, or was it...extreme discipline and unceasing solitary labor? 
* Cornell copying down into his journal a line from a Rilke biography: “In the letters written between 1910 and 1914 we find Rilke (continually) expressing a longing for human companionship and affection, and then, often immediately afterwards asking **whether he could really respond to such companionship** if it were offered to him, and wondering whether, after all, **his real task might lie elsewhere**.”
* All we can do is **wonder over the imaginary solitude** of others, what others do when they are alone, how they deal with the vastness and ephemerality of the day, which I think is for me increasingly the meaning and crisis of art. 
* Rilke himself remains so far from being able to work at all times. He wishes he had no pleasurable memories of not working. Hours spent waiting, flipping through old illustrations, looking at some novel. If only it were enough, he writes, to have a dog and sit in front of a shop window for twenty years.

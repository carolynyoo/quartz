## Summary 
What exactly is hanging out? Why do we need to do it more? Can one 'hang out' in a non-physical place, e.g. through TV or internet? (short answer: no)

## How I discovered it
Heard Sheila Liming in Ezra Klein's podcast in 2023

## Who should read it?
I expected a book heavier in sociocultural analysis and citations like [[Saving Time]], but a lot of the writing was about the author's personal experience. I didn't mind that really, but good to set expectations on it being more of a blended memoir. 

## How it changed me
_Behavior, ideas, perspectives, emotional shifts_
* How the internet is the opposite of the sublime, because of how comfortable it is 
* Conditions of satiation: fellowship and food 
* * Modern dwelling originated in early 19th century (citation Wlater Benjamin). Focus on individual space led to focus on self and interiority, and thus fields like psychology, as well as interior decor. This led to a retreat from the public sphere (e.g. salons)
* "Hanging Out on the Internet" chapter was most intriguing: 
	* feeling of IRL experiences feeling inauthentic
	* compulsion to document a way of making sure one still physically exists
	* Kant's [[Sublime]] as counterpoint 
	* Search (internet/digital) vs browse (physical place)
* Hanging out requires time, risk, care, and created opportunity

## Summary (incl. main characters, stakes, setting)
Sally and Kathy are sisters living in Connecticut. One school morning Sally, Kathy, and her boyfriend Billy are late driving to school. He is speeding and swerves to miss hitting a deer, instead hitting a tree that kills Kathy (this happens about 1/3 through the novel).
The remaining story is mostly an examination of grief and the feeling of brokenness it will permanently create in a person, and the blurry distinctions between obsession and trauma bonding and love. Sally and Billy bond in their grief and fall in love without allowing themselves to accept it for years, until the very end. 

## How I discovered it (rec, why now)
I read Allison Espach's previous book _The Wedding People_, which also broached some heavy topics but was way lighter than this book. 

## How I felt (thoughts on writing, themes, plot, pacing)
* Really enjoyed the second person POV of Sally talking to Kathy. We get a much more intimate portrait of both Sally and Kathy through this, and the first third depicting their sisterhood was so real and relatable, especially as a shy younger sister myself. 
* I understand how the love story aspect of this book might be alienating, but I think it was an interesting narrative choice to really delve into what grief does—isolate people, even within the family, because it is so hard to look at one's own pain, let alone hold each other's. That element was perhaps played up because this is a novel, one that needs real stakes, but I'd argue that it's a worthy metaphor for the irreplaceable bond among survivors. 
* Sally's mother was the true tragic character of this book. Her depression and psychosis through her struggles with coping, which further drive the family apart, felt really painful to read. 
* The final act of this book was pretty weak, though I enjoyed the "Hurricane Kathy" conceit. Why did we need another romantic character introduced, just to highlight the emptiness she feels with any man except the one who would most specifically understand her and the shape of her grief?

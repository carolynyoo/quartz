## Summary (incl. main characters, stakes, setting)
Cora and Sam, both married to other partners, meet in a Hudson Valley parents group in a town like Beacon, NY. They feel attraction to each other and engage in a "will they won't they
 affair for the better portion of a decade before finally succumbing to it. 

## How I discovered it (rec, why now)

I may have seen it recommended in *A Cup of Jo*. Was intrigued by the Madame Bovary blurb. Something about affairs seemed salacious and fun to read as a pick me up

## How I felt (thoughts on writing, themes, plot, pacing)
The author employs a *Sliding Doors* style of alternate timelines, one where the couple is having an affair and the other isn't. She is pretty clear about which is reality and which isn't, but it can still get a bit tricky to keep track of. Never intentionally vague like Katie Kitamura's *Audition* however. 

Much was explored about the inevitable boredom of marriage, the preference for logistics, the exasperation and near contempt at a partner who doesn't seem to pull their weight. I enjoyed that the actual affair didn't happen until more than halfway in so the reader could really sink into why a person would toe the line of having an emotional affair without real proof as an avenue for thrill without guilt. 

Funnily enough once the couple actually started their affair it felt sad and pathetic! Talk about rose-colored lenses, the fantasy being the most exciting part—not the reality. Ultimately the author was trying to paint a picture of how affairs aren't automatic moral failings, that the universality of these naughty feelings exist in long term marriages. 

## Who would like it
Existential New Yorkers filed with ennui
Readers who enjoy character-driven novels exploring interiority and rationale
